Statutory Compliance Checklist for E-Commerce, MSMEs & Digital Platforms

🇵🇭 Essential Legal Checklist for Philippine Online Businesses
Under Philippine law, all commercial websites, digital marketplaces, and online stores operating in the Philippines must comply with the following statutory requirements:
1. Internet Transactions Act of 2023 (Republic Act No. 11967) Mandatory
• Mandatory registration with the DTI E-Commerce Bureau (ECB) Online Business Database.
• Prominent disclosure of: Registered Business Name, DTI/SEC Registration Number, Physical Office Address, Active Email, and Contact Numbers on all public web pages.
• Eligibility for and display of the Philippine E-Commerce Trustmark.
2. Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) & NPC Mandatory
• Publicly posted, comprehensive Privacy Policy detailing data collection, processing purpose, and storage duration.
• Designated Data Protection Officer (DPO) registered with the National Privacy Commission (NPC).
• Explicit user consent mechanisms for forms and cookie tracking.
• Standard 72-hour mandatory breach reporting procedure.
3. BIR Tax Compliance & Revenue Regulations (RR 16-2023 & RMC 8-2024) Mandatory
• Valid BIR Form 2303 (Certificate of Registration) for online commerce.
• Issuance of BIR-compliant electronic Sales Invoices or Official Receipts (OR).
• 1% withholding tax compliance for online marketplace operators and merchant aggregators.
4. Consumer Act of the Philippines (RA 7394) & DTI Price Act Mandatory
• All prices must be quoted in Philippine Peso (PHP) inclusive of VAT with no hidden fees ("No PM for Price" policy).
• Transparent Return, Exchange, and Refund Policies (strictly prohibiting unconditional "No Return, No Exchange" statements).
• Prohibition against misleading discounts, counterfeit goods, or deceptive advertising.
5. E-Commerce Act (RA 8792) & Cybercrime Prevention Act (RA 10175) Security Standard
• Enforceable electronic contracts and digital consent logs.
• Mandatory HTTPS/TLS encryption across all authentication, profile, and payment pathways.

STATUTORY REQUIREMENT What are the mandatory Philippine government requirements for operating a website or e-commerce store?
▾

Under Philippine laws and national e-commerce regulations, all commercial websites, digital marketplaces, online storefronts, and digital service providers operating in the Philippines must comply with five core statutory requirements:

1. DTI or SEC Registration Certificate: Sole proprietors must hold a valid DTI Business Name Certificate; corporations and partnerships must have an active SEC Certificate of Incorporation.
2. BIR Form 2303 & Electronic Invoicing: Must be registered with the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR Form 2303) under appropriate digital commerce PSIC codes, issue BIR-compliant Sales Invoices/ORs, and comply with 1% creditable withholding tax regulations (RR 16-2023).
3. LGU Mayor's Business Permit: A valid business permit issued by the Local Government Unit (City or Municipality) where business operations or fulfillment hubs are stationed.
4. Internet Transactions Act Compliance (RA 11967): Mandatory registration with the DTI E-Commerce Bureau (ECB) Online Business Database and full disclosure of corporate identity, physical location, and contact numbers.
5. NPC Data Privacy Compliance (RA 10173): Published Privacy Policy, appointment of a registered Data Protection Officer (DPO), and secure AES-256-GCM encryption of personal data.
RA 11967 What disclosures are mandatory on a website under the Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967)?
▾

The Internet Transactions Act of 2023 (Republic Act No. 11967) mandates that every digital platform, commercial website, and online vendor must prominently disclose the following details on all public web pages (typically within the website header or footer):

  • Registered Business Name: Exactly as registered with DTI or SEC.
  • Official Registration Numbers: DTI Certificate Number or SEC Company Registration Number.
  • Physical Office Address: Verified physical address of the principal office or brick-and-mortar hub (not merely a P.O. Box).
  • Active Contact Details: Operational telephone hotlines, mobile contact numbers, and official business email address.
  • E-Commerce Trustmark: Display of the government-recognized Philippine E-Commerce Trustmark once verified by accredited registries like VeriPinoy.
⚠️ Failure to disclose these statutory identifiers constitutes an unfair or deceptive sales act punishable by administrative fines and website blocking by the DTI Secretary.
BIR & TAXATION What are the BIR tax compliance and e-invoicing rules for online stores (RR 16-2023 & RMC 8-2024)?
▾

Under BIR Revenue Regulations No. 16-2023 and Revenue Memorandum Circular No. 8-2024, online merchants and freelancers must abide by the following tax directives:

  • Valid BIR Form 2303: Merchants must upload and maintain an active Certificate of Registration reflecting online sales or digital service activities.
  • Issuance of Invoices: Electronic Sales Invoices or Official Receipts (OR) must be generated for all online sales transactions, detailing the Tax Identification Number (TIN) and itemized VAT breakdown.
  • 1% Creditable Withholding Tax: Digital marketplace operators (e.g., e-commerce platforms, payment aggregators) are mandated to withhold a 1% tax on 50% of the gross remittances to online sellers/merchants, unless exempted by BIR annual gross threshold limits.
RA 10173 & NPC How must a Philippine website comply with the Data Privacy Act (DPA 2012) and the NPC?
▾

The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) enforced by the National Privacy Commission (NPC) requires websites handling user accounts, orders, or sensitive personal data to implement:

  • Comprehensive Privacy Policy: Clearly stating the purpose of data collection, processing mechanisms, third-party sharing policies, and data retention schedules.
  • Designated Data Protection Officer (DPO): An appointed and NPC-registered DPO responsible for organizational privacy compliance.
  • Explicit Consent Checkboxes: Opt-in mechanisms on registration and checkout forms before any personal data is gathered.
  • AES-256 Encryption at Rest: Cryptographic security protecting sensitive identification documents and payment tokens.
  • 72-Hour Breach Notification: Established protocol to report any security incident or personal data breach to the NPC within 72 hours.
RA 7394 & PRICE ACT What does DTI's "No PM for Price" policy and the Consumer Act require?
▾

Under the Consumer Act of the Philippines (RA 7394) and the Price Act (RA 7581), as reinforced by DTI Joint Administrative Orders:

  • Mandatory Price Display: All prices of goods and services displayed online must be clearly indicated in Philippine Peso (₱ / PHP) inclusive of VAT. Requiring customers to send private messages ("PM is key" / "PM for price") is strictly prohibited by law.
  • Clear Return & Refund Policy: Online sellers must provide transparent return, exchange, or refund terms. Unconditional "No Return, No Exchange" statements are illegal under Philippine consumer law if goods are defective, misrepresented, or damaged.
  • Truth in Advertising: Prohibiting misleading promotional discounts, bait-and-switch pricing, or fake countdown timers.
RA 8792 Are digital milestone contracts and electronic signatures legally binding in the Philippines?
▾

Yes. Under the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000 (Republic Act No. 8792) and the Supreme Court Rules on Electronic Evidence, electronic documents, milestone agreements, digital consent logs, and cryptographically verified signatures hold the same legal validity, admissibility, and enforceability in Philippine courts as traditional paper contracts.
